# Board Design and Facilitation

Apply the current Miro composer style guidance, the user's chosen style, and
the existing board's conventions. Accessibility and legibility take priority
over decoration. This reference defines usable structure, not a fixed theme.

## Choose the artifact type

| Type | Purpose | Content rule |
|---|---|---|
| Workshop workspace | People contribute, discuss, learn, or decide | Structure plus a few labeled seeds; leave substantial working room |
| Synthesis artifact | Communicate supplied evidence or actual decisions | Populate with source-backed content; retain uncertainty and dissent |
| Technical working map | Inspect relationships or behavior | Explicit semantics, level of detail, boundaries, and open questions |

Use the composer guidance's workspace defaults: at most 2-4 labeled example
items per activity and no more than roughly one third of a frame occupied by
generated content. Do not fill participant spaces with imaginary contributions.
These are design defaults, not Agile framework requirements.

## Style is more than color

Ask what the board needs to feel and do:

- Facilitative: inviting prompts, low density, equal contribution space.
- Decision-focused: options, criteria, uncertainty, authority, and decision record.
- Technical: precise semantics, readable diagrams, contracts, constraints, and links.
- Stakeholder synthesis: evidence-to-outcome narrative with concise implications.

Then agree visual tone, language, expected detail, and whether to preserve the
existing theme. Default to current Miro guidance when no preference exists.
Do not impose Porsche branding, a rainbow sticky palette, or a designer's
personal style on every role.

## Build a navigable structure

1. Provide a start area: objective, audience, intended output, facilitation mode,
   evidence boundary, and navigation.
2. Separate context/evidence, working activities, decisions, and follow-up.
3. Use one activity or coherent dataset per frame. Avoid nesting superframes
   or wrapping the whole board in a decorative container.
4. Use a clear sequence or explicit spatial relationship. Align related items
   and repeat consistent card, lane, and frame patterns.
5. Preserve the user's selected target and surrounding work. For new sections,
   choose the approved area or let the create tool handle placement.
6. Link actual frames and relevant source artifacts using real URLs. Add a
   return-to-start link when a multi-frame sequence makes navigation harder.
7. Keep facilitator details distinct from participant instructions; a frame
   needs a short actionable instruction, not the entire playbook pasted onto it.

### Workspace frame contract

Each activity defines its question, input, output, contribution mode, proposed
duration, decision rule, and facilitator intervention. Express only the essentials
on the canvas; keep extended facilitation notes separately if the user wants them.

Use blanks for participants, source-linked notes for supplied evidence, and
"Example" labels for AI-generated seeds. Do not preassign people's answers.

## Accessible visual hierarchy

- Make section titles readable at overview zoom and working text legible when
  participating. Enlarge or split a crowded frame instead of shrinking text.
- Use supported fonts and a consistent hierarchy; follow the loaded ramp and
  spacing guidance instead of inventing unsupported typography.
- Pair every meaningful color with text, shape, placement, or a label. A red
  sticky alone must not mean "high risk" without another cue.
- Use adequate text/background contrast. Do not claim WCAG compliance merely
  because chosen colors look readable; follow applicable requirements and checks.
- Keep connector direction and labels clear; avoid ambiguous crossing arrows.
- Provide a textual summary or ordered alternative when a visual-only activity
  would exclude participants. Ask about input/assistive-technology needs.
- Keep sensitive identities out of shared notes. Do not call a normal Miro board
  anonymous or assume voting is private.

## Facilitation sequence

### Open

State the goal, contribution norms, working agreements, evidence boundary, time
budget, and decision authority. Allow clarifying questions before divergent work.
Time budgets in playbooks are suggested agendas, not mandatory framework rules.

### Diverge

Use individual reflection or asynchronous contribution before group discussion
when dominance or anchoring is likely. Capture one thought per note; preserve
the contributor's wording. Offer an alternative to rapid writing.

### Clarify and organize

Ask what a note means before rewriting it. Cluster by meaning, not by author or
visual similarity; retain originals and source links. Do not erase dissent or
outliers to make the board tidy.

### Converge

Choose a decision method before voting or ranking. Separate popularity from
evidence, feasibility, value, and authority. Dot voting can focus discussion,
but does not automatically decide product scope, accept risk, or establish truth.
Record abstentions, disagreement, and missing information when relevant.

### Close

Capture actual decisions, rationale, owner if agreed, next step, review point,
and how success will be checked. Read back the result for correction. Do not
assign an owner or due date that no one accepted.

## Reusable records

### Decision

Question | Options | Criteria | Evidence | Authority | Decision/status |
Rationale | Dissent/risks | Agreed owner | Review trigger

### Experiment

Problem | Hypothesis | Smallest test | Participants/data | Success/failure signal |
Constraints | Agreed owner | Observation window | Result/learning

### Action

Action | Why it matters | Agreed owner | Next step | Review point |
Evidence of completion | Status

### Evidence

Observation | Source/link | Context/date | Limitations | Interpretation |
Confidence/unknowns | Next question

## Anti-patterns and recovery

| Symptom | Better intervention |
|---|---|
| Empty input but polished conclusions | Return to a scaffold; label unknowns and seek evidence |
| Dominant voices decide everything | Add silent contribution, structured turns, and explicit authority |
| Retrospective becomes blame | Focus on situations, effects, and controllable system changes |
| Endless ideation | Restate the decision and criteria; timebox further exploration |
| Votes are mistaken for research | Label preference separately and design a validation step |
| Dense frames nobody can edit | Split activities, simplify copy, enlarge working areas |
| "Done" column hides quality risk | Expose completion policy and missing evidence |
| Cleanup threatens others' work | Preview exact edits; preserve originals or stop |

## Final design check

At overview zoom the purpose, sequence, and output areas should be apparent.
At working zoom the prompts, notes, controls, and connector meanings should be
readable. Confirm both using available board evidence; disclose when actual
rendered inspection is unavailable.
The artifact should make a useful behavior possible, not merely look complete.
